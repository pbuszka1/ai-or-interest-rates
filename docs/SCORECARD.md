## Bottom line so far

- **Pre-registered verdict:** Verdict 4, can't tell yet. Test B passes and shows no change; Test A fails its pre-trend check, and Verdict 3 needs both to pass. The label rests on a pre-trend test near its threshold (p from 0.067 to 0.107 across versions of the industry score), so the estimates are the better guide.
- **Industry employment:** a -5.0% slide since 2022 in AI-exposed industries, about 40% of it the pandemic-era boom unwinding. The design fails its pre-trend check. The dot-com comparison is suggestive only.
- **Early-career hiring:** a real tilt away from young workers in exposed industries and occupations, but it started in 2020–21, before ChatGPT. A stable gap can't rule out AI preventing a recovery.
- **The one AI-consistent signal:** young workers' share in the most exposed occupations widened further in 2026. One partial year of noisy survey data, and the estimate ranges from about -1% to -11% across specifications, -0.9% when young and older workers are compared within the same cells (`results/young_worker_robustness.json`), so worth watching, not concluding.
- **What the designs can and can't see** (injection tests, `results/injection_test.json`): the industry design detects a 10% AI-timed cut 100% of the time and dates it correctly. The two age designs (QWI, CPS) can't reliably detect cuts under about 20–24%, so "no post-ChatGPT widening" there is weak evidence either way. That's where more data (the ACS) would help.

## How the injection tests work

Random control units (no AI-exposure shock) are relabelled as treated, a known cut is planted in their outcome from 2023-Q1, and the exact pipeline is rerun, 100 times per size. That measures bias, power, false alarms and whether the method dates the change correctly, using the real data's noise. Each draw uses half the control pool, so the real designs (with more units) are somewhat more powerful than these numbers, especially QWI.
