## Occupation-code break in 2020

From 2017 to 2019, the standard deviation of the log change in the 60 largest groups' employment shares was 0.048. From 2019 to
2021 it was 0.096. Some of that is the pandemic and some is coding. These moves are nearly uncorrelated with exposure (0.04), so
they look like noise rather than a bias toward either answer. The largest was business operations specialists (group 13-11, +0.25).

## CES

- All series are seasonally adjusted, and a spot check of 10 series matched current FRED values exactly.
- No Test A industry is nested inside another.
- Test A's 94 industries cover 86.7% of published total private employment in 2015 and 85.5% in 2026.

## QWI

- The code uses age groups A03 (22–24) and A05 (35–44), which are the correct bands.
- One live spot check against the Census API (California, NAICS 5415, 2019-Q1) matched the saved hires exactly. This is one cell, not a full check.

## Exposure

- Spot checks match the source file, including software developers at 0.79 (GPT-4) and 0.05 (human) on the alpha measure.
- The highest and lowest groups are plausible: statistical assistants and writers at the top, construction helpers and janitors at the bottom.
- Occupation scores use 2020–25 weights to map Census codes to OCC2010, because both codes exist only from 2020. Using 2020–22 weights moves no GPT-4 score by more than 0.01.
